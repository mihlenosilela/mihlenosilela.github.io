# 1. Agile Team Charter

An Agile team charter is a living, shared agreement that sets out a team’s purpose, values, working agreements and boundaries so that daily work runs with less friction. This is our team’s own charter. [Download the original charter (PDF)](/assets/PRP_AGILE_CHARTER.pdf).

## Evidence

### Purpose, objective and vision

Our objective was to design, produce and sell affordable, high-quality ICT-branded clothing and merchandise (T-shirts, hoodies, caps and accessories, priced between R120 and R240) for students in the ICT programmes at CPUT. The problem we set out to address is that many students who rely only on NSFAS living allowances cannot afford suitable clothing for campus. Our four aims were to relieve that financial pressure, build departmental identity and unity, improve attendance and confidence, and run a viable, scalable student-run micro-business.

Our vision statement: *“To empower every ICT student at CPUT with affordable, identity-driven branded apparel that builds pride, fosters unity, and removes the financial barrier between a student and their full participation in campus life.”*

Our brand name is **Item Hive**. The original charter was written under an earlier working name, **ICT Threads**, which is why that name appears in the PDF.

### Values and principles

| Value | How it shows in practice |
|---|---|
| Collaboration | Daily stand-ups, joint design reviews and open feedback |
| Respect | Listening actively, acknowledging effort, resolving disagreements constructively |
| Transparency | Progress updates on Teams and WhatsApp, documented sprint outcomes, early reporting of blockers |
| Accountability | Meeting deadlines, regularly updating the Kanban board, following through on commitments |
| Innovation | Unique ICT-branded designs and improving usability through iterative testing |
| Empathy | Affordability and inclusivity in product and pricing decisions |
| Continuous improvement | Retrospectives after each milestone |

Our Agile principles: customer involvement, embrace change, people over process, and maintain simplicity.

### Working agreements

| Area | Agreement |
|---|---|
| Communication | Microsoft Teams for formal discussions and planning, WhatsApp for quick updates and urgent messages, and face-to-face meetings for design decisions |
| Daily stand-up | Monday to Friday at 18:00. Each member says what they completed, how far their tasks are, any blockers, and what they plan next |
| Expectations | Attend on time, communicate progress honestly, respond within a reasonable time, and warn the team if you cannot attend |

## Reflection

<div class="reflection" markdown="1">

**What.** As a team of six we wrote a charter that set our purpose, gave each member a role, and agreed on values, communication and a daily 18:00 stand-up. My role in the charter was Editor, Tester and Database Developer: I review and edit the team’s documents, test the system for errors and bugs, and help with the database design.

**So what.** The agreements that helped most were the clear roles and the daily stand-up, because everyone knew what they owned and what to report. The planned dates were harder to keep. The charter planned development to finish by 3 September and deployment to run from 8 September to 1 October, but our logbooks show that we were still integrating and debugging the backend in late September and redeploying the backend on Render in October, so the plan slipped by several weeks. Our survey also reached 22 students against a planned 30. The value we found hardest to live up to was accountability for the board. The charter promised that we would update the Kanban board regularly, but we were focused on building and testing and often forgot to move cards to Completed as we finished them. We then moved many cards in bulk, so the board’s dates do not show exactly when the work was done, and our checklists, logbooks and card owners are the better evidence of progress. This showed me that a charter only works when its agreements are checked regularly, not just written down.

**Now what.** Next time I would review the charter at the end of every sprint, build realistic dates with buffer for integration and testing, and make moving a card part of the daily stand-up so the board is always current.

**Connect.** The stand-up rule produced the entries in my [Agile Logbook](/agile-logbook), and the roles decided who owned each task in the [User Stories](/user-stories-and-tasks) and the [Sprint Backlog](/sprint-backlog).

</div>
